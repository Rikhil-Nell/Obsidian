## What This Round Is

A 15-minute recruiter pre-screen. **Not technical.** Goal on their side: confirm you're real, articulate, genuinely interested, and not a geography/comp/availability problem. Goal on your side: be memorable enough to get passed to the founders, and surface no red flags.

15 minutes means 2-3 real questions. Don't ramble. Tight, specific, done.

---

## Likely Questions + How To Hit Them

### "Tell me about yourself / walk me through your background"

Lead with builder identity, not student label. 30-45 seconds, not 3 minutes.

> I'm a final-year ECE student, but my real work is startup engineering. I build the systems underneath AI products — production voice AI running 500 to 600 calls a day on real telephony, an 8-agent backend at a restaurant startup serving 80+ cafes, a pipeline over a million call logs. I learn by picking problems that are slightly too hard and going through the wall instead of around it. That's why the "Hardcore Engineer" title is what made me apply — it's literally how I describe myself.

### "Why DualEntry? / Why this role?"

Be honest about the domain, strong about the systems.

> Two things. One, the title — hardcore engineer, dirty problems, ownership when things break. That's my operating mode. Two, the actual problems are harder than the pitch: a migration engine that maps messy legacy schemas, ledger integrity where silent wrong data is unacceptable, multi-entity correctness at scale. I'm honest that accounting isn't my home domain yet, but data correctness and systems that can't fail quietly is exactly the kind of thing I'm drawn to. The finance is learnable; the systems instinct is the hard part and I already have it.

### "What's your availability / situation?" (the real filter)

This is probably why a recruiter screen exists. Answer cleanly.

> I'm in my final semester, no courses left that block full focus. I can work effectively now and full-time on graduation. The role is remote-India eligible which is why it fits. Visa/NYC relocation down the line works for me if that's the path.

Know your number: comp band is $200K-$400K base + equity, but it likely differs for India remote. If asked salary expectations, don't anchor low or high blind — say you're optimizing for the problem and the team at this stage and you're flexible on structure, ask what band they have for the India remote tier.

### "What are you working on right now?"

Have one crisp answer. Backend Primitives / Accord build track, or the voice/agent work. Show you build even when no one's paying you to.

---

## Your Questions To Ask (have 2 ready)

- "What does the migration engine actually look like under the hood — is the hard part the schema mapping, the data validation, or the volume?" (signals you think about their real problem)
- "For someone joining now, what's the first thing that breaks that you'd want them to own?" (signals ownership appetite)
- Avoid: comp-only questions, perks, anything that reads as low-intensity.

---

## Red Flags To Avoid

- Don't oversell into fiction — they ship fast and will catch vagueness later. Specifics only.
- Don't apologize for being a student. State it once, move to shipped work.
- Don't bury the finance-domain honesty — name it briefly, frame as learnable, pivot to systems strength.
- Don't ramble. 15 minutes. Tight answers, then stop talking.
