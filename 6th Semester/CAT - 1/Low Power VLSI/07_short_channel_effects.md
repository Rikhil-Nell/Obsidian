# Short Channel Effects

## Learning Objectives
After this section, you will understand:
- What defines a "short channel" MOSFET
- All five short channel effects and their mechanisms
- How these effects impact power dissipation and performance
- The relationship between SCE and leakage

---

## What is a Short Channel Device?

A MOSFET is considered **short-channel** when the channel length $L$ is comparable to the depletion layer widths of the source and drain junctions.

| Device Type | Channel Length | Characteristics |
|-------------|----------------|-----------------|
| Long channel | $L \gg x_{dD}, x_{dS}$ | Ideal behavior, good gate control |
| Short channel | $L \approx x_{dD}, x_{dS}$ | Non-ideal effects dominate |

**Technology Examples:**
- Long channel: ≥ 1 μm (older technologies)
- Short channel: ≤ 45 nm, 7 nm, 3 nm (modern technologies)

---

## Origin of Short Channel Effects

SCE arise from two physical phenomena:

1. **Limitation on electron drift characteristics** in the channel
2. **Modification of threshold voltage** due to shortened channel

The five primary short channel effects are:

```
Short Channel Effects
├── 1. DIBL and Punchthrough    ← High Power Impact
├── 2. Surface Scattering       ← Moderate Impact
├── 3. Velocity Saturation      ← Performance Impact
├── 4. Impact Ionization        ← Reliability Impact
└── 5. Hot Electron Effect      ← Reliability Impact
```

---

## 1. DIBL (Drain-Induced Barrier Lowering)

### Mechanism

In a MOSFET, the source-channel barrier controls subthreshold current. In short-channel devices:

1. Drain bias creates a depletion region that extends toward source
2. High drain field penetrates to the source-channel junction
3. Source-channel barrier is **lowered** by drain voltage
4. More electrons can cross → increased subthreshold current

![[dibl_energy_bands.png]]

### Long Channel vs Short Channel

| Condition | Long Channel | Short Channel |
|-----------|--------------|---------------|
| Drain field | Localized near drain | Extends to source |
| Barrier control | Gate only | Gate + Drain |
| $V_T$ dependence on $V_D$ | Negligible | Significant |

### DIBL Effect on $I_D$-$V_G$

![[dibl_id_vg_curve.png]]

- DIBL shifts the $I_D$-$V_G$ curve to the left
- Increases OFF-state current (leakage)
- $V_T$ effectively decreases with increasing $V_D$

### DIBL Enhanced By:
- Shorter channel lengths
- Higher drain voltages
- Lower doping

## Punchthrough

### Mechanism

When drain-source voltage is high enough, the drain and source depletion regions **merge** through the channel bulk.

![[punchthrough_diagram.png]]

**Result:** Current flows independently of gate voltage (even at $V_G = 0$).

### Punchthrough Conditions

1. Short channel length $L$
2. Low substrate doping
3. High $V_{DS}$
4. Depletion regions touch

$$x_{dD} + x_{dS} \geq L \implies \text{Punchthrough!}$$

### DIBL vs Punchthrough

| Aspect | DIBL | Punchthrough |
|--------|------|--------------|
| Location | At surface | In bulk (subsurface) |
| Gate control | Partially maintained | Completely lost |
| Current path | Through inverted channel | Through depleted bulk |
| Onset | Gradual | More abrupt |

## 2. Surface Scattering

### Mechanism

In short-channel devices with high longitudinal electric field ($\varepsilon_y$):

1. Strong vertical field ($\varepsilon_x$) confines carriers to a thin inversion layer
2. Carriers collide with the rough Si-SiO₂ interface
3. **Surface mobility** is reduced compared to bulk mobility

$$\mu_{surface} \approx \frac{1}{2} \mu_{bulk}$$

![[surface_scattering.png]]

### Impact

- Reduced current drive capability
- Degraded transconductance
- Performance limitation

---

## 3. Velocity Saturation

### Mechanism

At low electric fields, carrier velocity increases linearly with field:
$$v_d = \mu \cdot \varepsilon_y$$

At high electric fields (> 10⁴ V/cm):
- Velocity increases more slowly
- Eventually saturates at $v_{sat} \approx 10^7$ cm/s

![[velocity_saturation.png]]

### Key Values

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| $\varepsilon_{crit}$ | ~10⁴ V/cm |
| $v_{sat}$ | ~10⁷ cm/s at 300K |
| Saturation field | ~10⁵ V/cm |

### Impact on Drain Current

In long-channel devices:
$$I_{D,sat} \propto (V_{GS} - V_T)^2 \quad \text{(quadratic)}$$

In velocity-saturated short-channel devices:
$$I_{D,sat} \propto (V_{GS} - V_T) \quad \text{(linear)}$$

**Result:** Reduced transconductance in saturation.

---

## 4. Impact Ionization

### Mechanism

High-energy electrons near the drain can cause:

1. Electrons gain energy from high electric field
2. Carriers collide with silicon atoms
3. **Electron-hole pairs** are generated by impact ionization

![[leakage_overview.png]]

### Consequences

- Holes flow to substrate → parasitic substrate current
- Region between S/D acts like NPN transistor base
- If hole current causes 0.6V drop: S-B junction forward biases
- Electrons injected from source → creates more e-h pairs
- Can lead to **latch-up** or **snapback breakdown**

---

## 5. Hot Electron Effect

### Mechanism

High-energy ("hot") electrons:
1. Gain energy from high electric field
2. Cross the Si-SiO₂ barrier (3.1 eV for electrons)
3. Get **trapped in the oxide**

![[hot_electron_injection.png]]

### Consequences

| Effect | Result |
|--------|--------|
| Oxide charging | $V_T$ increases over time |
| Gate control degradation | Performance degrades |
| Reliability issue | Device aging |

**Key Insight:** This is primarily a reliability issue, causing device degradation over time.

---

## Summary: Power and Performance Impact

| Effect | Power Impact | Performance Impact |
|--------|--------------|---------------------|
| DIBL | High (increases leakage) | $V_T$ reduction |
| Punchthrough | High (loss of control) | Loss of switching |
| Surface Scattering | Low | Reduced mobility |
| Velocity Saturation | Low | Reduced transconductance |
| Impact Ionization | Medium (substrate current) | Reliability |
| Hot Electrons | Medium (gate current) | Reliability |

---

## Mitigation Techniques

### For DIBL/Punchthrough
- Halo/pocket implants
- Higher substrate doping
- SOI (Silicon-on-Insulator)
- FinFET structure

### For Hot Electrons
- Lightly Doped Drain (LDD) structures
- Lower supply voltage
- Graded junction profiles

### For Velocity Saturation
- Supply voltage scaling
- Accept reduced transconductance
- Adjust device sizing

---

## Common Mistakes

1. **Confusing DIBL and punchthrough** - DIBL is surface effect, punchthrough is bulk
2. **Ignoring SCE in power analysis** - Major source of increased leakage
3. **Assuming ideal square-law** - Velocity saturation changes this
4. **Not considering reliability** - Hot electrons cause long-term degradation

## Self-Check Questions

<details>
<summary>1. What is the key difference between DIBL and punchthrough?</summary>

**DIBL:** 
- Surface effect at Si-SiO₂ interface
- Drain lowers source barrier
- Gate still has partial control
- Increases subthreshold current

**Punchthrough:**
- Bulk effect through substrate
- Depletion regions merge
- Gate has NO control
- Current flows independent of gate
</details>

<details>
<summary>2. Why does velocity saturation cause linear I-V relationship?</summary>

In velocity saturation:
- Carrier velocity = constant ($v_{sat}$)
- $I_D = W \cdot Q_{inversion} \cdot v_{sat}$
- $Q_{inversion} \propto (V_{GS} - V_T)$
- Therefore: $I_D \propto (V_{GS} - V_T)^1$ (linear)

Contrast with long-channel square-law: $I_D \propto (V_{GS} - V_T)^2$
</details>

<details>
<summary>3. How do short channel effects increase power consumption?</summary>

- **DIBL:** Lowers $V_T$ → exponentially higher subthreshold leakage
- **Punchthrough:** Uncontrolled current even when "off"
- **Impact ionization:** Creates substrate current
- **Hot electrons:** Gate leakage current

All these increase standby power, which is critical in modern low-power designs.
</details>
